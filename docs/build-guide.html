BRAIDBENCH / ENGINEERING STUDY 01

From moving spools
to a braided cord.

A modular tabletop braider using laser-cut plywood, printed rotors and bobbins, two small stepper motors, and an ESP32.

Prototype status — read before fabrication. This is an original, dimensioned design study with editable CAD and prototype fabrication files. It has not been built. The circular track and single-pin carrier do not yet positively select the outgoing path at a gear handoff. That is an unresolved mechanism, not merely an assumed printing tolerance. Develop a guided shoe / transfer insert or adapt a proven carrier before powering the assembly. The model also leaves brackets, fastener retention, guards and final tension hardware to workshop detailing. Do not treat the complete machine as a fabrication release.

The first useful build is a hand-operated two-rotor transfer test. If a carrier follows the intended branch repeatedly, the rest of the frame, drive, take-up and controller become worth assembling. If it circles the same rotor, jams or needs a nudge, change the guide geometry before scaling up.

Two small experiments before the full machine

Start with the carrier guidance coupon to compare a round foot and a pointed shoe at a straight crossing. Then use the tension calibration board to measure bobbin braking, slider friction and two-leg loop storage. Both have their own laser/print files, editable CAD, interactive model and hand-test instructions. Neither is a drop-in completion of the main carrier: a curved-track two-rotor handoff test and integration are still required.

How the mechanism makes a braid

  1. The motor turns a 20-tooth pinion. It drives one 40-tooth rotor. Adjacent rotors mesh and turn in opposite directions; the full gear ring must contain an even number of rotors.
  2. Each rotor has four open carrier pockets. A carrier travels around a rotor, then needs to transfer into the neighbouring rotor at a tangent point. The fixed track and a positively guided carrier must choose the outgoing route. This last constraint is not resolved by simply cutting tangent circular grooves.
  3. The moving yarns converge at the forming eye. Their changing positions create crossings. There is no spinning hook that merely twists all the strands into one bundle.
  4. A second motor pulls the product through rollers. Its feed per rotor revolution changes crossing spacing. A constant-diameter roller is easier to calibrate than winding directly onto a reel that grows in diameter.

Printing and laser setup

  1. Cut fit-coupon.svg first. Measure openings with callipers and test actual bearings, rod and a printed carrier neck. Establish a CAM kerf offset once; the SVGs contain nominal outlines with no kerf compensation.
  2. For the first trial, print rotors and bobbins in PLA or PETG at 0.2 mm layers, 4 perimeters, around 40% infill. These are starting settings, not tested load ratings. Print each rotor gear-down / bore vertical; inspect the upper bearing shoulder and the gear overhang.
  3. The bobbin’s upper flange needs support, or split the model into printable halves and join concentrically. The carrier has downward-facing features; orient and support them, then finish the neck and drive-pin surfaces.
  4. Ream or carefully finish bearing seats and spindle bores to match actual parts. Do not force oversized bearings into a brittle print. Detail axial retention so neither rotor nor bearing can climb off its axle.
  5. Remove char and rough edges from track surfaces. Use a replaceable low-friction wear layer only after rechecking neck length and axial clearance. Laser-material settings belong to the specific machine and stock.

Build in stages

  1. Prove one handoff by hand. Print rotors 01 and 02 from the 8-strand set, two axle spacers and one carrier. Mount their axles 80 mm apart on scrap. Use the corresponding local section of the track study. Add the carrier with an empty bobbin and turn slowly through both directions. Observe whether it takes the required branch without losing support. Design a two-point / shaped guide shoe and transfer insert if needed; the current single-pin carrier cannot guarantee this. Do not power this test.
  2. Resolve bearings, retention and the upper track. In the CAD, base top is Z=6, spacer top / lower bearing bottom Z=40, gear Z=48–56, upper bearing Z=57–64, horn disk Z=64–70, track Z=76–82. The retaining disk sits below the track and the carrier foot above it. Clamp the bearing inner races with a 10 mm metal spacer between bearings and a 12 mm metal spacer above the upper bearing, up to the track underside. Their 12 mm outside diameter clears the 14 mm horn centre hole. Shim the lower stack to the measured plate thickness; do not clamp the rotating outer races. Maintain running clearance without vertical escape. The circular track islands mount on stationary rotor axles; the separate central island mounts on four 70 mm M3 standoffs. Countersink its top fasteners flush after laser cutting so carrier feet can pass. All islands remain stationary.
  3. Assemble the gear ring. Cut the chosen base. Fit the four masts, feet and rotor axles. Install the indexed rotors in order, viewed from above: station 01 is on +X (right); later stations proceed counterclockwise. Match the CAD tooth/horn phases. Turn the full loop by hand before fitting carriers; it must turn without a tight spot.
  4. Fit the drive motor. The 66 mm motor riser stands on four 34 mm spacers above the 6 mm base. Mount the motor with four M3 screws on its 31 mm pattern. The 20T pinion drives station 01 at 2:1 reduction. Fix it to the 5 mm shaft with a correctly prepared grub screw; check shaft flat, axial engagement and phase.
  5. Install carriers and bobbins. Resolve the guide shoe first. Fit a retained spindle, felt drag washer, bobbin, light spring and adjustment nut. Attach and smooth each yarn guide. Set similar drag using a spring scale at a full and nearly empty bobbin. A friction brake has changing yarn tension as package radius changes; it is not a constant-tension dancer.
  6. Fit the forming guide and take-up. Support the guide at Z=380 on the rear masts. Mount the upper frame at Z=470 and bridge at Z=476. Use two metal angle brackets to secure the vertical motor face. Fit driven roller, silicone sleeve and idler; adjust the slotted idler axle to grip without crushing. The pictured braid exits upward at the nip; add a smooth downstream guide and collection container.
  7. Enclose the moving parts and wire controls. Add a physical guard around gears, bobbins and the roller nip, plus a lid and strain relief on the electronics. These guards and brackets are not fully designed in this package. Wire emergency stop / guard interruption of the motor bus before allowing software to enable motion.
  8. Thread and commission at low speed. With motor power disconnected, lead each thread from its bobbin through its guide, then gather the threads through the forming eye and rollers. Hand-turn complete cycles with light tension. Only after reliable handoffs, no interference and verified power interruption, qualify the mechanism and enable firmware. Start around 1 rotor rpm and adjust take-up while observing the first short sample.

DRV8825 carriers accept 3.3 V STEP/DIR logic. Place a local electrolytic across each driver’s VMOT/GND, with polarity correct; the proposed 100 µF / 50 V part exceeds Pololu’s 47 µF minimum. Set current limit to the actual motor and cooling arrangement. On the genuine Pololu 0.1 Ω-sense board, I_limit = 2 × VREF; clone sense resistors may differ. Follow the driver manufacturer’s wiring and current-limit instructions and Espressif’s board documentation.

Starter firmware and pitch control

braidbench.ino provides low-speed STEP/DIR control, a coupled acceleration ramp, latched input faults and explicit arming. There is no wireless control. MECHANICS_QUALIFIED is deliberately false in the supplied file because the transfer geometry is unresolved. Set it true only after the mechanical commissioning steps above are satisfied.

Open the sketch in Arduino IDE, install Espressif’s ESP32 board support and select the actual WROOM board. No additional motion library is required. Hardware-specific compilation / flashing and bench execution have not been performed here; see the validation report for the narrower host-side checks.

Serial monitor: 115200 baud, newline
RESET              clear a fault only when all NC contacts are closed
ARM                enable the driver outputs; no motion yet
RUN 1 25           1 rotor rpm; 25 mm take-up per rotor revolution
STOP               stop immediately and disable outputs
STATUS             print state, requested rotor rpm and feed ratio

RUN limits: 0.2–5 rotor rpm; 1–100 mm per rotor revolution.
After any stop/fault: RESET if needed, then ARM again.
The physical emergency stop cuts the motor bus independently.

For a 200-step motor at 1/8 stepping, there are 1,600 STEP pulses per motor revolution. A 20T pinion driving a 40T rotor requires 3,200 drive pulses per rotor revolution. With a nominal 40 mm capstan, one take-up revolution moves approximately 125.66 mm. Measure the roller with its sleeve under load before relying on that number.

drive STEP/s   = rotor_rpm × 3200 / 60
take-up STEP/s = rotor_rpm × mm_per_rotor_rev × 1600 / (60 × π × effective_diameter_mm)

Feed per rotor revolution is an operational ratio, not automatically braid pitch or picks per inch. Those also depend on the actual carrier trajectories and crossing repeat. Mark 100 mm on a test length, measure real travel and adjust CAPSTAN_DIAMETER_MM. Record yarn, loading, speed, feed ratio and a photo of the resulting braid.

Engineering work still required

  1. Positive carrier guidance at every transfer: develop the guided shoe and crossing geometry. This is the main feasibility gate.
  2. Complete the carrier: guide-post attachment, retained spindle, bobbin brake, yarn eye and anti-lift behaviour. Test at full and empty spool conditions.
  3. Qualify mechanical interfaces: bearing fits / retainers, axle clamping, gear backlash, motor brackets, pinion and capstan set screws, idler slot retention and mast stiffness.
  4. Detail and fit guarding: enclose gears and nip points; implement physical motor-power interruption, driver cooling and strain relief.
  5. Establish the braid: confirm actual crossing sequence on a short sample, calibrate feed, then develop any 1-over-1, 2-over-2 or flat-cartridge variant.

Files and design provenance

cad/ contains editable FreeCAD assemblies and STEP interchange files. The source of dimensions is tools/generate_cad.py; rerun with Python and FreeCAD’s Python modules installed. FreeCAD files contain named solid parts; regeneration from the script changes dimensions. They are not a fully constrained feature-history assembly. models/ contains GLB and viewer data; GLB uses metres and Y-up, while CAD uses millimetres and Z-up. Firmware and fabrication files are local and versionable.

The mechanical geometry and program are original for this project. External sources establish mechanism precedents and component interfaces; they do not validate this design. No third-party braider CAD has been copied into the package. Three.js is vendored under its included MIT license.
